# Paid-order verification

The core tests exercise mixed goods/services, independent commitments, fixed total versus deposit/balance, finite recurring cycles, indefinite subscriptions, zero-initial later/conditional fees, calculated prices and exact localized money. They reject incomplete line coverage, inconsistent totals, changed quote/wording hashes, hostile JSON, getters, unsafe text, unresolved production guarantees, wrong product associations and changed original label bytes.

Controller tests cover concurrent/reentrant submission, observer exceptions, presentation checks at dispatch, quote expiry, host rejection, lost responses, retained recovery after expiry, incompatible contexts, mismatched outcomes, timeouts, late completion, destruction and request-ID allocation failure. DOM tests keep critical facts expanded, use the exact native payment label, block failed required images and changed facts/ARIA wording, and hide unavailable recovery actions. Client hashes and DOM checks are integrity measures, not proof of legal compliance or a substitute for host authorization.

Site tests cross the HTTP/session-DO boundary: independent visitors cannot access each other’s quotes or history; repeated requests create one retained admission; exact reviews and separate payment/contract/confirmation states appear in a searchable history. Direct host tests exercise expired/superseded quotes, idempotency before expiry checks, release changes and malformed/rebound requests. The example does not charge money or form contracts.

Primary research includes current CRD Article 8(2) and its Article 6(1)(a), (e), (la), (o), (p) references; full Conny judgment paragraphs 44–56 and the operative part; and indexed Fuhrmann-2 primary excerpts, including paragraphs 28 and 34.
